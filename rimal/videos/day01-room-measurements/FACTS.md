# Facts — "5 measurements that make any room look designed"

Every number on screen and in the voice-over traces to a row here. All five are design rules of thumb (guidelines, not building code), and the film presents them that way.

| # | Claim as shown / said | Source | Quoted wording | Checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hang art with its centre 145 cm (57 in) from the floor: "the 57-inch rule" | Apartment Therapy, "Use the 57″ Rule To Hang Pictures at the Perfect Height" (apartmenttherapy.com/how-to-hang-artwork-properly-57-inches-from-the-floor-6174); Framebridge, "What is the right height to hang a frame?" | "hang it at 57″ on center"; "the vertical center … is at about 57 inches above the floor" | 2026-11-26 |
| 2 | Coffee table 40–50 cm (16–20 in) from the sofa | Sit Design, "Sofa, Coffee Table and Rug Proportions" (sitdesign.com/blogs/news/…) | "40–50 cm between the front of the sofa and the table edge, close enough to reach a cup" | 2026-11-26 |
| 3 | Pendant 75–90 cm (30–36 in) above the dining table | Lightology lighting guide; Quoizel, "A Guide to Pendant Light Hanging Heights" | "approximately 30 to 36 inches above the table surface"; "a common guideline … 30 to 36 inches above the surface" | 2026-11-26 |
| 4 | 60 cm (24 in) of table width per seat | Lamon Luther, "Table Size & Space Guidelines"; Magnolia Lane, "Dining Table Room Layout" | "allowing 24 inches of space per person"; "approximately 60 cm (24 inches) of table width per chair" | 2026-11-26 |
| 5 | Walkways at least 90 cm (36 in): "the kitchen planners' minimum" | NKBA Kitchen Planning Guidelines with Access Standards (media.nkba.org/uploads/2022/05/Kitchen-Planning-Guidelines.pdf) | "Walkway … Recommended: be at least 36″ (914 mm)" | 2026-11-26 |

## Rounding
- 57 in = 144.8 cm, shown as 145 cm.
- 30–36 in = 76–91 cm, shown as 75–90 cm.
- 36 in = 91.4 cm, shown as 90 cm. The NKBA wording is "at least", so the film says "at least".

## Illustrative (no label needed beyond the drawings being plainly diagrams)
- All rooms, furniture and art are flat diagrams, not a real project. Dimension lines are drawn to the stated value, not to scale.

## Not claimed
- That these are legal requirements or building codes.
- Any statistic about how many designers use them.
