## 1. What we are making

| Item | Goes on | What it carries |
|---|---|---|
| **Rail clip** | Stair handrails (Ø30–50 mm), flat, oval and timber corridor rails, Ø32 mm grab poles | Tactile ribs (Information / Safety / End of stairs), braille, a raised number, a glow strip |
| **Door tag** | Lever door handles (classrooms, stair-core doors) | Braille, a raised number or room name, a glow strip |

Each install is measured on site and made to fit (DALIL-facts §3).

## 2. The main idea: a hard face and a soft grip

One material can't be both "soft" and "strong to read". So we use **two materials joined together**:

- **Hard face (outside):** carries the braille, numbers and ribs. Braille has to stay sharp for years under thousands of hands, so it must be hard.
- **Soft grip (inside):** a rubber-like layer that touches the rail. It grips without slipping, doesn't scratch the rail, takes up small differences in size, and feels soft.

In mass production this is called **overmoulding** or **2-shot moulding**: the soft rubber is moulded straight onto the hard part, so they can't be pulled apart. For prototypes, a dual-material 3D printer can print both together, or the two parts are printed and bonded.

## 3. Materials

| Part | Material (recommended) | Why | Don't use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard face and body | **ASA** (3D printing, UV-stable) → later **PC or PC/ABS** (moulding), safety yellow | Holds its shape in the sun: heat limit about 85–98 °C. UAE rails in direct sun can reach 70–80 °C. Doesn't yellow or crack in UV. | **PLA** (softens at 55–65 °C: will bend in the sun). **PETG** only indoors (softens about 65–75 °C). |
| Soft grip liner | **TPU** (thermoplastic polyurethane), hardness **85A–95A** → moulding: **TPU or TPE** | Rubbery, strong and tear-resistant, grips without glue, doesn't mark the rail, resists cleaning chemicals | Silicone (very hard to bond to plastic), cheap foam (crushes and gets dirty) |
| Glow strip | **Strontium aluminate** glow pigment (yellow-green), in a clear insert or mixed into the face material | Much brighter and longer-lasting than old zinc-sulphide glow. Needs no battery: it charges from room light. | Glow paint on the surface (wears off under hands) |
| Braille dots | Part of the hard face (moulded or printed in), domed | Hard dots don't wear flat | Soft TPU dots (wear and feel mushy); stick-on dots (picked off) |
| Lock (anti-tamper) | **Stainless steel** tamper screw (pin-Torx head), or a one-way plastic ratchet | Can only be removed with a special bit, or by cutting | Normal Phillips screws (anyone can undo them) |
| Adhesive (flat rails, extra hold) | **Acrylic foam tape (VHB-type, 3M)**, cut to shape | Very strong, weatherproof, survives heat, invisible inside the clip | Ordinary double-sided tape (fails in heat) |

**Fire:** interior fittings will need a fire test. Ask the manufacturer for **flame-retardant (FR) grades** of both materials (for example UL 94 V-0 rated, `[VERIFY]` with the lab). Choose FR materials now so certification doesn't force a redesign later.

**Colour vs glow:** glow pigment is pale yellow-green in daylight, while our brand colour is safety yellow `#FFC700`. Keep the body yellow and make the glow a separate strip or edge, so neither is weakened.

## 4. How it stays on (very hard to rip off)

Use three locks together, so no single one has to do everything:

1. **Shape lock (main):** the clip wraps **more than halfway** around the rail (about 200–240°) and snaps on. The soft TPU liner squeezes the rail, so it can't slide or twist. Pulling it off needs far more force than a hand sliding past.
2. **Hidden lock:** after it snaps on, it is closed with **one tamper screw** (pin-Torx, under the clip, out of reach) **or** a **one-way ratchet** like a cable tie. It can only come off with DALIL's tool, or by cutting.
3. **Glue for flat or odd rails:** acrylic foam tape inside, where wrapping isn't possible.

**Door tags:** a soft TPU strap goes round the lever handle (like a watch strap), with the hard reading plate on top. It closes with a **one-way ratchet**, and the spare tail is cut off. It can't be undone, only cut.

**Targets to test** (proposed; set real numbers after testing):
- Pull-off force with bare hands: more than `[≥ 200 N, TO CONFIRM]`.
- Doesn't twist under a hand's grip.
- No movement after 10,000 hand slides.

## 5. Not disturbing everyday users

- **Thin:** sits no more than about **4–6 mm** above the rail surface `[TO TEST with users]`.
- **Ramped ends:** a gentle slope (about 15–20°) at both ends, so a hand slides over it and doesn't catch or bump.
- **No sharp edges:** all edges rounded (at least 1 mm radius). Nothing for sleeves or bags to snag on.
- **Doesn't block the grip:** short (about **60–80 mm** along the rail) `[TO TEST]`. On door handles, it sits at the hinge end of the lever, away from where the hand pushes.
- **Easy to clean:** smooth, with no deep gaps, so dirt doesn't collect. Must survive alcohol and chlorine wipes (hospitals) and hand sanitiser.
- **Hygiene:** antimicrobial additives exist. **Don't claim** them until a lab has tested them.

## 6. Braille and raised numbers (the numbers the mould must hit)

From the ADA signage standard (US, 2010, §703.3), widely used for signs. Check which standard the UAE approval will use (ISO 17049 / unified Arabic braille: `[VERIFY]`).

| Measurement | Range |
|---|---|
| Dot base diameter | 1.5–1.6 mm |
| Dot height | 0.6–0.9 mm, domed (rounded) |
| Distance between dots in one cell | 2.3–2.5 mm |
| Distance between cells | 6.1–7.6 mm |
| Distance between lines | 10.0–10.2 mm |

- Braille text is checked by a braille reader before any customer order (spec §4).
- Raised numbers: high contrast, raised about 0.8 mm `[VERIFY standard]`, plain font.

## 7. Parts list (what each product needs)

**Rail clip:**
1. Hard body / reading face: ASA → PC, yellow, with ribs, braille and number moulded in.
2. Soft grip liner: TPU 85A–95A, overmoulded or bonded.
3. Glow strip: strontium aluminate insert.
4. Lock: 1 stainless pin-Torx screw, or a one-way ratchet.
5. Optional: acrylic foam tape pad (flat rails).
6. Label inside: batch number and date, so faults can be traced.

**Door tag:**
1. Hard reading plate (ASA → PC), braille, number or room name, glow strip.
2. Soft TPU strap with one-way ratchet.
3. Label inside: batch and date.

**Installer kit:** pin-Torx bit and driver, rail gauge and calipers (to measure rails), alcohol wipes (to clean before fitting), cutter for strap tails, spare clips.

## 8. How we make it: now and later

| Stage | Method | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Now: prototypes and first buildings** | 3D printing. FDM with ASA + TPU (dual-material printer with an enclosure), or resin printing for very sharp braille | Custom fits, small numbers, fast changes |
| **Later: volume** | **Injection moulding with overmoulding** (hard + soft in one part) | Low cost per part, same quality every time. The mould is a big upfront cost `[ASK]`; a few standard sizes cover most rails, with inserts for the text. |

Workshop equipment for the 3D-print stage (Month 1 "workshop and printers ready"):
- enclosed printers that can print ASA and TPU (dual material is best);
- a filament dryer (TPU and ASA absorb moisture);
- calipers;
- a shore-A durometer (rubber hardness);
- a force gauge (pull-off test);
- a glow / luminance meter, or the lab can do this.

## 9. Tests before selling (record results in `test-log.csv`)

1. Pull-off and twist by hand (target above).
2. Slide-past: 10,000 hand slides, then check braille sharpness.
3. Heat: 80 °C for 24 h, no bending or loosening.
4. UV: weeks in sun or a UV chamber; colour and strength.
5. Cleaning: alcohol, chlorine and sanitiser wipes, every day for a month.
6. Glow: brightness after 10 and 60 minutes of darkness (lab standard `[VERIFY]`).
7. Fire: at an accredited lab (certification tracker).
8. Users: blind and low-vision readers check finding, reading and comfort (`dalil-accessibility`).

## 10. Questions to ask the manufacturer today

1. Can you do **overmoulding** (hard PC + soft TPU in one part)? Have you made similar parts?
2. Which **flame-retardant** grades of PC and TPU do you use or recommend? Do you have fire test reports?

4. Can the braille dots be moulded within **±0.1 mm**? How do you check that?
5. Can you mould in **glow pigment**, or add a glow insert? Which supplier?
6. Can one mould take **swappable inserts** for different text and numbers?
7. Can you supply the **tamper screws** and the **one-way ratchet straps**?
