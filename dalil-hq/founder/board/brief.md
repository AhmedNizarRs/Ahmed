## The idea

DALIL ("guide" in Arabic, دليل) is a Sharjah, UAE startup founded at the University of Sharjah. It makes a small 3D-printed clip that snaps onto an existing handrail or handle and turns it into a wayfinding sign people can read by hand.

Line it uses: "Make the existing path speak." No app. No battery. No renovation.

## The problem it names

- 21.8 million people in the Middle East and North Africa live with moderate-to-severe vision impairment (the company's figure).
- Floor numbers, exits and room names are usually on walls, where a hand cannot find them. The handrail is already in the hand, and it is blank.

## The product

- **How it works:** Clip (snaps on in seconds, no tools), Find (a hand sliding along the rail meets it without searching), Read (braille and a raised number, e.g. "Floor 2. Exit left.").
- **Tactile grammar:** ribs on the clip tell the hand the type of message before reading it: Information (lift ahead, prayer room, rooms 201 to 215), Safety (ground floor, exit, turn left), End of stairs.
- **Every level of sight:** braille, with Arabic braille built in, for people who are blind; high contrast and raised numbers for people with low vision; a photoluminescent glow for everyone when the lights fail.
- **Where it fits (one module, same grammar):** stair handrails Ø30–50 mm, classroom and tower door lever handles, hospital corridor rails (flat, oval, timber), metro carriage poles and platform rails, bus grab poles Ø32 mm, airport rails, mall balustrades.
- Each install is measured on site and printed to fit. Colour: safety yellow.
- Product spec is at v0 (draft): material, glow pigment and performance, braille dimensions, durability results, print time, material cost per clip and failure rate are all [TO CONFIRM]. No durability tests logged yet.

## Price and economics (company figures)

- AED 130 per clip or door tag (the working quote rate). Per-floor rate and care-plan price: [TO CONFIRM].
