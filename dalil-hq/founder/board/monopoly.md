# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- **A real insight about where the sign goes.** The Monopoly lens asks for the founders' secret. Here it is: the handrail is already in the hand, and it is blank. Wall signs make a person search; a clip on the rail meets the hand. That is a change in kind, not a cheaper version of an existing sign. It is the one place this idea could be ten times better than the next option, and it is still untested.
- **A small market it could own first.** Starting on the founders' own campus, then Sharjah, then the UAE is the right shape. A narrow first market (Arabic-braille wayfinding on rails in Sharjah buildings) is one where a two-person company can take a big share early.
- **Local know-how.** Arabic braille built in, a three-type tactile grammar (Information, Safety, End of stairs) and a glow for blackouts are specific to this region and this use. If the grammar became the accepted local convention, that would be a brand and a standard, the most durable thing available here.
- **The founders already split the work.** Product and production with one founder, sales with the other. Distribution has an owner from day one.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **No moat against a copycat in month six.** The product is a 3D-printed clip with braille on it. Printing is cheap and easy to get, design and patent protection are [TO CONFIRM], and the price gap with wall signs (AED 130 against AED 180–400) is less than three times, not ten. Any signage maker who already sells to the same facilities teams could add a rail clip. Nothing in the brief stops that today: no network effect, no scale advantage, no locked-in standard. Open question: does rail-mounted braille already exist, from whom and at what price? **/founder-competitors** answers this.
2. **No proven way to reach the buyer.** The pipeline is one target, the founders' own university, and nobody there who can sign has been found yet. No pilots, no letters of intent, no community partner contacted, waitlist sign-ups [TO CONFIRM]. Facilities buyers usually buy to meet a rule, and certification has not started (which approvals and which authority: [TO CONFIRM]). Without it, the product is a nice-to-have competing for a maintenance budget. The plan to reach 22 buildings by month 24 has no cost-to-win attached. Open question: what does it cost, in time and money, to win one building? **/founder-cfo** answers this, and **/founder-consumer** tests whether facilities buyers would pay.
3. **The ten-times claim is unproven, and the spec is a draft.** No user testing with people who are blind or have low vision yet. No durability tests. Glow performance, material, failure rate and cost per clip are all [TO CONFIRM]. The stated 58% margin cannot be checked while material cost is unknown. If people do not find and read the clip faster than a wall sign, or it fails on a hospital rail after a year, the secret is gone. Since it carries Safety messages, a failure costs trust, not just a refund.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Has anyone already put braille on handrails, here or abroad? If so, why did it not spread, and what do you know that they did not?
- Do local codes require wall signs regardless? If so, are you an add-on, and does the hospital comparison (AED 87k against AED 118k–263k) still hold?
- What stops a signage supplier already selling to your buyer from copying the clip after your first install?
- Who at the University of Sharjah can say yes, and when will you meet them?
- How does the first building pay you, and what does it cost you to win it?
- Which certification, from which authority, and what does it cost and how long does it take?
- Can the tactile grammar become a standard others have to follow, through a community partner or an authority?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Proof of the secret:** one round of user testing with people who are blind or have low vision, showing the clip is found and read without searching, faster than a wall sign.
2. **One real buyer:** a named decision-maker and a signed pilot or letter of intent from the University of Sharjah or another first building.
3. **A path to a moat:** design or patent filing started, the certification route and authority named, and one community partner engaged on the tactile grammar.
4. **Numbers that check:** cost per clip and cost to win a building from /founder-cfo, and the competitor map from /founder-competitors, before any money is committed.
