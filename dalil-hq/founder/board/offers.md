# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Low effort for the buyer.** "No app. No battery. No renovation." Clips snap on with no tools, and the price is per floor with everything included: site visit, measuring, custom-fit clips and door tags, installation and an accessibility map. That is a done-for-you offer, which is where the Offers lens wants it to be.
- **Fast results.** The company says install takes 1 week, against about 6 weeks for wall tactile signs. On the value equation, time-to-result is a real advantage.
- **A stack is forming.** Measuring, install, map and a yearly care plan already turn a part into a service. The glow in a blackout and Arabic braille built in are differences the buyer can see, not just a lower price.
- **Room to charge more.** The hospital figure (AED 87k against AED 118k–263k plus installation for wall signs) suggests the price could go up and DALIL would still look like the obvious choice.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood is close to zero today.** Nothing proves it works: the spec is a v0 draft, there are no durability tests, no user testing with people who are blind or have low vision, no certification (not started; which authority is [TO CONFIRM]), no pilots and no customers. A facilities team buying a safety and wayfinding product needs proof before anything else. If the clips wear, come loose, lose their glow or fail an approval, the offer falls apart.
2. **The person who pays may not feel the pain.** The pain is real for the end users, but the buyer is the building owner or facilities team. The brief does not say what makes them buy this year: a regulation, a tender requirement, an inspection or a reputation goal. Without that trigger, nobody is in a hurry and sales cycles will drag. Worse, if a code requires wall signs anyway, DALIL becomes an extra cost on top of them, not a cheaper replacement, and the main comparison stops working.
3. **The pitch leads with being cheaper, and the margin is not proven.** The comparison table opens on AED 130 against AED 180–400. The Offers lens treats "cheaper" as a commodity position. And the stated 58% margin sits next to a material cost per clip, print time and failure rate that are all [TO CONFIRM], for an offer that includes a site visit, custom printing and installation. The margin is a claim until /founder-cfo builds it from real costs.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who signs the purchase order at the University of Sharjah, and what makes them say yes this year rather than later?
- Does any UAE or Sharjah rule require tactile wayfinding? Would DALIL meet it, or would it sit next to wall signs the building must still buy?
- Why would a facilities manager believe the clip lasts? What is the plan to prove durability, glow and braille readability, and when?
- What is the guarantee? What happens if a clip breaks, comes loose or fails an inspection?
- What do one clip and one floor really cost to deliver, site visit and install included? (/founder-cfo)
- What do named competitors charge, and what do their customers complain about? (/founder-competitors)
- Would buyers pay more than wall signs for the glow and the faster install? (/founder-consumer, then real buyers)
- How many buildings are on the waitlist? It is [TO CONFIRM].

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Proof of likelihood.** Finish at least the first round of user testing with people who are blind and people with low vision, and log durability tests on the real material, before any paid sale.
2. **A named buyer trigger.** Write down the regulation, tender or mandate that makes a building buy, and confirm whether DALIL replaces wall signs or comes on top of them.
3. **One committed pilot.** A signed letter of intent or a paid pilot from a decision-maker, starting with the University of Sharjah.
4. **A rebuilt offer that does not lead on price.** Lead with "found by the hand, works in a blackout, installed in a week." Add a guarantee the CFO's numbers can afford (for example, free replacement under the care plan) and a clear name for the per-floor package.
5. **A margin built from real costs.** Replace the stated 58% with a figure from /founder-cfo, worked out from the actual cost per clip, print time, failure rate and installation labour.
